🟢 Business Understanding: Cynefin Framework (Model)

Cynefin is a "sense-making" model that helps mentally place a problem in the right context and choose an appropriate approach to solving it. It shows that there is no one universal approach.

#4 basic domains (contexts in which we work)

According to the context and its typical characteristics, we choose the approach.

  • Clear / Obvious: Clear rules, no degrees of freedom (tightly constrained).

    • Approach: Sense - Categorize - Respond (routine approach). The procedure is clear in advance and e.g., documented.
    • Result: Best practice.

  • Complicated: The system is tightly coupled, cause-effect relationship exists, but only an expert can reveal it.

    • Approach: Sense - Analyze - Respond (analytical approach). Algorithmic procedure, scientific methodology, the correct approach leads to the goal.
    • Result: Good practice.

  • Complex: Loosely coupled, consequences cannot be precisely predicted in advance. Software development domain.

    • Approach: Probe - Sense - Respond (agile approach, experimentation). Do not analyze without prior knowledge. Success is the result of evolution that cannot be precisely planned in advance. Rules can change during the process.
    • Result: Emergent practice.

  • Chaotic: Lacking any constraints (lacking constraint, de-coupled), crisis situation. The situation is unclear, the cause-effect relationship is unknown or undeterminable.

    • Approach: Act - Sense - Respond (immediate action for stabilization, crisis management).
    • Result: Novel practice.

Key rule and anti-pattern: Never apply "good practices" and analytical approach (BDUF, strict phase division) from the Complicated domain to problems in the Complex domain (such as SW development). It is a recipe for disaster.

#Example from practice: Industrial automation module and system integration

  • Clear (Obvious) Connection of a standard digital sensor with an existing driver to the system. There is a clear manual and parameter table for this. You look, categorize it into the right category and apply the known procedure (Sense - Categorize - Respond). You apply Best practice.

  • Complicated Design of the hardware filtering and stabilization itself for NAMUR inputs. This requires expert knowledge of electronics, load calculations and delays. Analytics work one hundred percent here because physical cause-effect relationships are clearly defined, only a layperson cannot calculate it (Sense - Analyze - Respond). You apply Good practice.

  • Complex Sporadic glitch of comparators manifested only during complex system tests. Software and hardware under load interact unexpectedly and desk analytics fail. You must create a technology Spike or deploy a software simulator of noisy signals (Probe), monitor how the system responds (Sense), and flexibly adjust the architecture accordingly, perhaps by inserting an empty filter (Respond). You apply Emergent practice.

  • Chaotic Critical device failure directly in the field at the customer's site, threatening to stop the production line or a safety incident. There is no time at all to analyze logs. You must immediately issue a quick hotfix that forcibly switches the system to safe mode or disables the faulty peripheral (Act). Only after stabilizing the situation do you find out what happened (Sense) and prepare a full fix (Respond). Novel practice emerges.
