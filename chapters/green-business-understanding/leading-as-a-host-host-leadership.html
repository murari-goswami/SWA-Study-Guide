🟢 Business Understanding: Leading as a host

#Transformation Hero > Servant > Host leadership

  • Hero: Traditional concept of a leader (king, movie hero) who pulls everything alone and wins. In an agile environment, this does not work because we want competent and independent teams (empowered teams).
  • Servant: A concept from the 70s (Robert Greenleaf), where the leader primarily serves the team and holds it together. It has fundamental limits: it creates the feeling that the leader has no power and responsibility. If the team is going the wrong way, the "servant" can hardly stop it. For example, the Scrum Master role requires responsibility for the process, which is difficult to enforce without authority.
  • Host: A metaphor (McKergow and Bailey), where the leader is neither a hero nor a servant. Functions as a host at a party – prepares a suitable environment and takes care of the "guests", but at the same time maintains authority to set rules (boundaries) and possibly intervene if someone is spoiling the party for others.

#4 positions (Where you are as a leader)

  • On the stage / In the spotlight: You are the center of attention. It is about public speaking, setting vision, team briefings or presenting results.
  • Among the people / With the guests: You are directly among people as one of many. You walk around the team, talk to them individually, find out their strengths, interests and connect with them.
  • On the balcony / At the balcony: A step back high above the level of action. You monitor the big picture (helicopter view) to understand the dynamics, and let things flow without direct intervention in details.
  • In the kitchen: Your backstage and private space. Here preparation, planning, self-reflection or learning takes place, out of sight of guests.

#6 roles (What you do as a leader)

  • Initiator: You give the initial impulse, take the first step and set things in motion.
  • Inviter: You invite motivated people to collaborate. You attract people to participate (invitation), you do not force it with authority.
  • Space Creator: You build and maintain a suitable physical and psychological environment in which ideas and events can emerge.
  • Gatekeeper: You define norms and boundaries. You protect the created space from disruption and ensure that agreed rules are respected.
  • Connector: You connect people and ideas. You connect those who would not otherwise collaborate, and then get out of their way so they can create.
  • Co-Participant: You are not just a passive organizer, but directly engage in the work (just as the host eats their own food with their guests).

#Example from practice: Application of Host Leadership in various roles

#1. Line management in matrix organizational structure

Context: In a matrix organization, the line manager does not manage the daily tasks of their subordinates (project managers do that). They take care of their development, evaluation and working conditions.

  • Space Creator & Gatekeeper: You function as a protective shield. You do not manage exactly what people program, but you remove obstacles from their way, solve their equipment and administration (Space Creator). At the same time, you protect them from external organizational noise and political pressures so that they have peace for the actual project work (Gatekeeper).
  • On the balcony: You do not sit with them at daily problem-solving in code. You gain perspective by systematically collecting and analyzing feedback from various project leaders across the company. You monitor overall trends in the performance and satisfaction of your people.
  • Among the guests: At regular personal meetings (1-on-1), you discuss with people their individual career needs, frustrations and motivation directly at their level, not from a position of authority.

#2. Leading a large strategic project (Modularization)

Context: This is a large initiative consisting of many smaller, relatively independent subprojects (domains). Each domain has its own leader.

  • On the stage: As the overall leader of the entire project, you are the face of the initiative. You set the vision, regularly present overall results to management and communicate progress to the rest of the company.
  • Gatekeeper: You give individual domain leaders great freedom and autonomy in execution. However, you set and uncompromisingly guard hard boundaries of collaboration: you require absolute adherence to agreed systematicity, planning deadlines and uniform reporting format.
  • Co-participator: To avoid detachment from reality and "managerial blindness", you do not keep just a coordination role. You take on leadership of one of the smaller subprojects yourself, directly participate in deliveries and face the same process obstacles as others.
