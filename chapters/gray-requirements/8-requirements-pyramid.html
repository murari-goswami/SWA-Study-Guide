4. Requirements Pyramid and multi-level Traceability

To avoid chaos in the enormous volume of data in complex projects, requirements engineering uses hierarchical layering, known as the Requirements Pyramid.

The hierarchical pyramid classifies data into four layers:

Pyramid level Requirement type Strategic significance and layer content Practical product example
Level 0 Business Goals (BG) Answer the strategic question "Why do we want to build this product?" Product vision and business model. "Manufacture a camera and sell 1 million units by the end of 2026 with a net margin of $35 per unit."
Level 1 Customer Requirements (CR) Answer the question "What do customers want?" Outside-in perspective. Capture real processes. "As a user I want to take a shot without having to focus manually."
Level 2 System Requirements (SyR) Answer the question "What exactly will we build?" Inside-out perspective. Architecture starts to take shape here. "When the shutter is pressed halfway, the electronics must focus in <0.4 seconds."
Level 3 Subsystem Requirements (SuR) A deeply technical level defining the precise behaviour of microservices, databases, or modules. Input for programmers. "Once focused, the shutter subsystem must react with a delay of <0.05 seconds."

While the product manager focuses on Business Goals and Customer Requirements, the software architect dominates at the level of system (SyR) and subsystem (SuR) requirements, where they are accountable for technical feasibility.
