🟡 Change Management and Fearless Change

#Innovation Adoption Curve

People do not adopt changes all at once. They are divided into groups:

  • Innovators (2.5%) & Early Adopters (13.5%): This is where you must start. You must convince this group.
  • Chasm: Critical breaking point between enthusiasts and the rest of the company.
  • Early Majority (34%) & Late Majority (34%): The mass that will join when they see it works.
  • Laggards (16%): Trying to convince the mass of laggards is a useless waste of time.

#Change Transition Curve

Every change triggers an emotional roller coaster. The architect must know what the team needs in a given phase:

  • Shock & Denial: People need Information and time for informal communication.
  • Blaming & Panic (Blaming, panic, insecurity): The curve falls down. Here Support is necessary.
  • Acceptance & Testing (Acceptance and experimentation): The curve rises. Only here comes Direction – defining goals, tasks and timeline. Subsequently Encouragement is needed.
  • Discovery (Discovery and integration): The change settles down. People need Feedback to consolidate goals.

#Fearless Change (Game / Framework for solving obstacles)

Methodology (from L. Rising) helps overcome resistance to change using a set of proven "patterns". The process has 3 steps:

  1. Describe change: Describe the change you want to introduce (e.g., introducing TDD).
  2. Brainstorm obstacles: Identify obstacles (e.g., no time, missing tools). Here it is useful to connect with Responsibility Process (not get stuck in excuses).
  3. Look for a change pattern: Find a pattern to overcome the obstacle.

#Examples of Change Patterns

  • Just Do It (Try it): You have no experience with innovation and don't know if it will catch on? Try it first yourself on your own work. You will get first-hand data about advantages and limits before you start spreading it further.
  • Do Food (Provide food): Turn an ordinary and impersonal meeting into a special event by simply bringing food.
  • Test the Waters: Before proposing a widespread change, do a small, bounded experiment with the team. When it doesn't work out, losses are minimal. When it works out, you have hard data in hand for further negotiation.
  • Step by Step: People are naturally afraid of giant transformations. Break your big vision into small, easily digestible pieces. Start with the absolutely simplest thing so the team experiences a quick success (so-called Quick Win).
  • Personal Touch: Don't try to "sell" the news with a mass email or big presentation in front of the whole department (there people easily unite in resistance). Go to key people personally and discuss it with them face to face.
  • Champion: Find someone in the organization who has great respect or formal power (Vested Authority). Inspire them with your idea and use their influence to help you break through and remove bureaucratic obstacles.
  • Brown Bag: Organize an informal lunch meeting (ideally combined with the Do Food pattern), where the news will only be casually discussed. People are much more open outside the stress of classic mandatory meetings.

#Example from practice: Introduction of AI coding tools in embedded environment

#Situation analysis through discussed models:

  • Innovation Adoption Curve

    • I chose the strategy "focus enthusiasts". I did not try to immediately break over the knee conservative embedded veterans (that is pragmatic Late Majority or outright Laggards).
    • I focused on Innovators and Early Adopters. My goal was to create successful PoC with them and obtain data to overcome the gap (Chasm) towards the rest of the company.

  • Fearless Change Patterns I applied patterns for conservative environment:

    • Test the Waters / Step by Step: I proposed doing small moderated experiments and PoC, not widespread transformation.
    • Just Do It: We applied it to concrete practical examples. With this I wanted to find out the real limits of LLM models in embedded (and show whether the skepticism of seniors is technically justified, or it's just fear).

  • Change Transition Curve & Leadership

    • The team (and at times I too) was in the phase of Panic / Dread (fear of the future) and Insecurity.
    • Management statements (threat that there will be no work for refusers) were a textbook example of Passing unfiltered heat from above. This destroyed psychological safety and drove people into defense.
    • In the role of Host Leader, I had to function as a shield (Defend the team). I knew that on the curve, a huge dose of support (Support) is now needed. I am solving this through mentoring and coaching – I created a safe environment where developers could try AI tools without fear of punishment for mistakes.

  • Responsibility Process

    • Conservative developers got stuck in the Justify phase (excuses about circumstances: no OS, small stack, real-time). In part they were technically right, but in part it was a subconscious defense against leaving the comfort zone.
    • My goal through PoC was to take away these arguments for Justify. When agents write valid and safe code even for no-OS environment, developers will have no choice but to move to the Responsibility phase and accept a new way of working.
