🟡 Self-leadership and Responsibility Process

#Why deal with self-leadership at all?

  • You will get more of what you really want.
  • You will be less frustrated and angry.
  • You will be clearer about what is important to you.
  • You will stop getting stuck in the role of "victim" and will have real impact more often.

#Covey's circles (Energy focus)

  • Circle of Concern: Things that annoy you, but you have no control over them.
  • Circle of Influence: Things where you do not have full control over the outcome, but you can influence it by what you control.
  • Circle of Control: What you control directly (your thoughts, words, actions).

#The Responsibility Process

It is not just a character trait, but a mental process that works the same for everyone. It is triggered automatically with any problem or upset. The mind gradually offers you various excuses (coping states), and taking responsibility means consciously rejecting these states.

#Process levels

  1. Denial: Ignoring the problem. The subconscious protects you from being overwhelmed.
  2. Lay Blame: Finding the culprit in another person. (External focus)
  3. Justify: Excuses about circumstances (weather, processes, lack of time). (External focus)
  4. Shame: Blaming yourself. You feel like victim and perpetrator at the same time, you think you are stupid. (Internal focus)
  5. Obligation: You do things because you "have to", not because you want to. You feel like a victim of circumstances and do not see the possibility of choice. (Internal focus)
  6. Quit: Parking the problem, procrastination. Trying to avoid the pain of Shame or the weight of Obligation.
  7. Responsibility: Full assumption of power to create and choose your own response.

#3 keys to responsibility (Three Keys to Responsibility)

  1. Intention: I want to respond responsibly when something goes wrong.
  2. Awareness: I can catch myself in lower phases (excuses, blaming) progressively earlier.
  3. Confront: I face the truth so I can learn and improve.

#Catch Sooner Game (Training)

is a mental training for building the second key to responsibility – awareness (Awareness). The goal is to progressively shorten the time your brain drowns in excuses (Justify) or blaming others (Lay Blame).

The loop works as follows:

  • Catch: You catch yourself that you have fallen into a defensive state.
  • Change: You consciously leave this state and move your mind toward responsibility.
  • Forgive: You forgive yourself and do not blame yourself, because it is an automatic brain reaction (if you did not do this, you would fall straight into the shame and guilt phase – Shame).
  • Vow: You make a resolution that next time you will catch yourself in this excuse again a little earlier. The more you train this loop, the faster your brain rejects these states.

#Example from practice: Self-analysis through Responsibility Process

Situation: A junior architect encounters passive resistance when trying to push through refactoring. What does the passage of their mind through defensive states (Responsibility Process) look like?

  • Upset (Trigger): Developers block changes, management pushes for absurdly fast result.

  • Denial: "It will settle down, they will definitely understand at the next meeting." (Ignoring reality).

  • Lay Blame: "Those developers are just lazy and sabotaging it. Management is clueless." (Energy is in Circle of Concern – pointing finger out at others).

  • Justify: "The framework is so historically intertwined that we do not have capacity for it. Besides, no one gave me a formal mandate." (Excuses about circumstances, processes and state of things, still Circle of Concern).

  • Shame: "I am probably incompetent as an architect, I cannot sell it, I am doing it wrong." (Focus has flipped inward, blaming himself and feeling stupid).

  • Obligation: "I have to continue organizing those Conceptboard workshops and endure it because I have it in my goals and I have no choice." (Stuck in victim role, doing it from coercion).

  • Quit: "I don't care, I will not convince anyone anymore. I will rather go write some of my side tasks." (Procrastination, escape from frustration from obligation).

  • Responsibility: "I have no control over their resistance or the assignment (Circle of Concern). But I have full power over what project I do (Circle of Control). I will ask for a transfer to another team where my work makes sense."

How does your role in sparring fit into this? When the architect was going in circles with you, he was probably stuck in phases Lay Blame and Justify. You can get him out of this precisely with a confrontational question: "How do you yourself contribute to this frustrating situation continuing?" By this you force him to leave Circle of Concern and shift attention to Circle of Control – and realize that his staying in a non-functional setup is something he can change himself.
