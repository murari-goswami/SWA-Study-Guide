🟡 Peer Feedback

The basic model for providing and receiving feedback stands on three steps: Perception - Effect - Wish.

#Providing feedback

  • Perception: Focus on what you actually saw and heard.
  • Effect: Describe what impact it had on you and how you felt about it.
  • Wish: Say what behavior you would wish for in a similar situation next time.
  • Key rules for providing:
    • Speak for yourself – use "I", avoid general phrases ("one should", "it is said").
    • Speak directly to the person, not about them in third person.
    • Remember that one positive comment often has a greater effect than ten negative ones.

#Receiving feedback

  • If the context is not clear to you, ask about the specific situation.
  • Try to understand how the other person felt.
  • Find out what behavior they would wish from you next time.
  • Key rules for receiving:
    • Take feedback as an opportunity for growth.
    • If you don't have the capacity to receive it right now, ask for another time.
    • Listen attentively.
    • Essential thing: Do not defend yourself, do not explain and do not justify yourself.

#Examples from practice: Application of the Perception – Effect – Wish model

1. Cross-team collaboration with external stakeholders (Constructive feedback for internal architect)

  • Perception: "I noticed that when we were working with the external team, after the initial hesitation and objections from our internal team, the external stakeholders became passive and stopped arguing – they quickly fell into acceptance."
  • Effect: "It seemed resigned to me, and I am concerned that we are unnecessarily losing authority and influence within the cross-team collaboration."
  • Wish: "I would like you to try to decode their objections more with open questions next time and not discourage them right at the beginning, so that we keep the external team actively engaged."

3. Regular 1-1 from Team Leader position (Burnout prevention)

  • Perception: "I registered that this week you have already taken over operational tasks for colleagues for the third time, even though you have enough of your own work."
  • Effect: "It worries me, we are risking your exhaustion and threatening your main priorities."
  • Wish: "I would need you to refuse such requests next time and refer them to me for prioritization."
