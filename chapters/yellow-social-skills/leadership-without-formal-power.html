🟡 Leadership (without formal power)

#Leadership vs. Management

  • Management: Organization of processes, planning and executive control of work.
  • Leadership: Inspiring and motivating people to achieve a common goal, supporting creativity, innovation and purpose.
  • A software architect functions as a leader without formal power (has no subordinates). Does not manage by orders, but by communication and vision.

#Two types of authority

  • Vested Authority:

    Official role with defined power and responsibility (manager, director). What the organization gave you, it can take away at any time.

  • Natural Authority:

    Authority that you have built yourself with your competencies, practice and personal qualities (ability to communicate, resolve conflicts). You take this authority with you wherever you go.

#How to influence your Leadership Status (Dos & Don'ts for architects)

  • Increase (How to grow):

    • Lead by example
    • Active listening (listen more than talk)
    • Share know-how
    • Build relationships
    • Defend the team
    • Let people shine
    • Offer vision in the form of discussion and story.

  • Decrease (How to destroy it): Micromanagement and excessive control, ignoring feedback, pushing your own solution by force (Push for solution), arrogance, blaming the team, passing unfiltered heat from above.

The list of how to increase your leadership status (Dos) essentially defines the principles of Host Leadership (and Servant Leadership). The leader in this role does not function as a micromanager or dictator, but as a host. Like throwing a good party:

  1. You create space and invite people: You do not tell guests exactly what to talk about, but you ensure safe and stimulating conditions for them. These are exactly your post-its: Open environment, Empower team or trying to convince through discussion (Offer a vision by discussion).
  2. You step back (Stepping back): Once the project (or party) is running, the host does not push into the center of attention. Lets their guests shine. In your materials, this is literally: Be the one in background, not the one who did, Let people shine or Highlight praise.
  3. You care and listen: A good host walks among people, asks and perceives what each person needs. Your key rule Active listening serves this purpose (a good leader listens more than talks).
  4. You intervene and protect (Stepping forward): You enter the action decisively only when a problem threatens or someone is spoiling the party. In practice, this means protecting your people. This is exactly what your post-it Defend the team covers and the warning against passing unfiltered stress from management (Passing unfiltered heat from above).

#Example from my practice: Community Python framework (Natural authority, Host Leadership)

This case demonstrates the application of Host Leadership principles and building Natural Authority in the architect role.

#Phase 1: Creating space and vision (Stepping forward)

  • Situation: The tester community was writing ad-hoc scripts. From the position of unofficial architect, I had no formal power (Vested Authority) to order a widespread change.
  • My action: I did not issue a directive. Instead, I shaped the discussion (Offer a vision by discussion), created a functional MVP and proposed a name. This was an application of Lead by example and Share your know-how principles, by which I was building natural authority (Natural Authority).

#Phase 2: Step back and hand over execution (Stepping back)

  • My action: I did not start owning the code and writing it for them. I handed over the MVP to enthusiasts from the community for rewriting into professional form. By this I applied Delegate, Trust others and Empower team.
  • My role: I moved into the reviewer role. In Pull Requests, I did not issue commands, but directed code towards SOLID principles. This corresponds to points Coaching and More active architecture reviews.

#Phase 3: Let others shine (Let people shine)

  • Result: I withdrew into the background (Be the one in background, not the one who did). The team took over the framework, continues to develop it and achieved real business success with it.
  • Summary: The result in practice confirmed the quote from Lao Tse: "When the effective leader is finished with his work, the people say it happened naturally."
