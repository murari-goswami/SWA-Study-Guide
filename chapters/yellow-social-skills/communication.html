🟡 Social Skills: Communication

#Basic principles

  • Weinberg's Second Law of Consulting: No matter how it looks at first sight technically, it is always ultimately a people problem.

  • Speed: The speed of communication directly determines the speed of the entire project. Software development is a cooperative game.

    The author of this concept is Alistair Cockburn. He does not look at development as an engineering assembly line, but defines it as follows:

    • Game principle: People do not write code in isolation. They use "markers and props" (code, tests, documentation, tickets, comments) to inform each other, remind themselves of context and inspire each other for the next move in the game.
    • End of game and residue: The goal of the current game is to get the software system running. However, the essential side product (residue) is precisely those created markers that must remain understandable for players of the next game.
    • Next game: The next game means future maintenance, system modification, its replacement or development of a related solution.

Main business impact:

From this definition follows a hard direct proportion (also shown on the graph in the presentation): project speed depends directly on communication speed. Therefore, reference is made here to Weinberg's second law of consulting: "No matter how it looks at first sight, it is always ultimately a people problem". When the transfer of "markers" between players is sluggish, technically perfect code will not save the project.

  • Mehrabian's rule: If words and body language are in conflict, we are guided 55% by facial expressions, 38% by tone of voice, and the words themselves constitute only 7% of the message.

#Four-ears communication model (Schulz von Thun)

The model reveals that every message carries information on four different levels simultaneously. Each person has "four beaks" for transmission and "four ears" for reception, which can have different sizes and sensitivity.

4 levels of message:

  • Factual content (Facts): Objective information and data (Is it true? Is it relevant?).
  • Self-revelation: What the sender reveals about themselves, their motives and mood.
  • Relationship level: How the sender relates to the recipient and what they think about them. It is transmitted mainly non-verbally (tone, facial expressions) and it is the most sensitive ear, which often interprets things as reproach.
  • Appeal: What the sender wants the recipient to do, think or feel (manifestly and hidden).

Why misunderstandings arise: The most common cause is level mismatch – the sender means something purely factually, but the recipient catches it with an oversensitive relationship ear. The absence of non-verbal signals in written text or unconscious transfer also contributes to misunderstanding.

How to communicate effectively:

  • Sender: Align verbal and non-verbal expression, avoid ambiguity and irony, empathize with the listener.

  • Recipient: Do not listen only with the "relationship ear", verify understanding, ask directly about the appeal and do not automatically take things as an attack.

  • Check-in & Check-back: Technique to reduce misunderstandings.

    • Check-in = I realize with what intention I am sending the message, possibly with what ear I am receiving it.
    • Check-back = validation. I say the intention explicitly out loud, or as a listener I ask ("Do I understand correctly that you want me to...?"). Ensuring that we are "on the same page".

#Johari window Model for understanding relationships and making communication more efficient. It has 4 quadrants:

One axis determines known/unknown to me, the other axis to others. This creates 4 combinations.

  1. Arena (Open space): Known to me and others. This is where efficient communication takes place.
  2. Blind spot: Known to others, but I don't know it about myself.
  3. Facade (Mask): Known to me, but I hide it from others.
  4. Unknown: Unknown to me and others.
  • Goal: Maximize "Arena". This is done by receiving feedback (reduces blind spot) and self-disclosure/sharing (reduces facade).

#Active listening

An excellent communication tool for overcoming resistance, implementing changes and revealing real problems. It has 3 steps:

  1. Build a relationship: Open posture, eye contact, nodding, showing interest.
  2. Clarify facts: Paraphrasing, asking about meaning.
  3. Deal with feelings: Empathy, mirroring. Being able to put yourself in someone else's shoes.
  • Most common mistakes: Exaggerating or ignoring emotions (phase 3), making up and adding own thoughts, rushing, psychoanalyzing the other, or dull "parroting" of words instead of paraphrasing.

#Host Leadership (in more detail in Business understanding)

Evolution of leader: from Hero through Servant to Host.

  • 6 roles: Initiator, Inviter, Space creator, Gate keeper, Connector, Co-Participant.
  • 4 positions: At the balcony (overview), In the kitchen (preparation), In the spotlight (on stage), With the guests (among people).

#Communication models in practice (FT D CZ)

Recruitment interviews (Self-presentation)

  • Johari window: The candidate presents their Facade. The goal is to use Active listening to break through this facade and enlarge the Arena.
  • 4-ears model: Turn on the Self-revelation ear. Do not just evaluate facts, but listen to what the candidate unconsciously reveals about their character and motives.

1:1 interviews and feedback

  • Johari window: With constructive feedback, you reduce the subordinate's Blind spot.
  • 4-ears model: Beware of the Relationship ear, people often interpret factual criticism as a personal attack. Always use the Check-back technique to verify how your feedback was understood.

Growth talks (Development discussions)

  • Host Leadership: You are not a hero who lines up a career. You are a Space Creator (you give space) and Inviter (you encourage initiative).
  • Active listening: Focus on step 3 (Working with feelings and empathy). Avoid jumping into speech and psychoanalysis so that the subordinate opens up.

Leading team meetings (Presentation and discussion)

  • Host Leadership: You dynamically alternate positions In the spotlight (presentation of assignment from above) and With the guests (facilitation of subsequent team discussion).
  • 4-ears model: With assignment from management, you must have an absolutely clear and understandable Appeal. The team must know exactly what action is expected of them.

Strategic projects and complex negotiations

  • Weinberg's law: When the debate gets stuck, remember that it is ultimately always a "people problem" (ego, fear).
  • Host Leadership: You function as a Connector (connecting quarreling parties) and Gate keeper (guarding project boundaries).
