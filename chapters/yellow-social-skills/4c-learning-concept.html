🟡 4C Learning Concept (Accelerated Learning)

The 4C model serves to accelerate the learning process and shorten the time between acquiring new knowledge and its application in practice. It is based on how the human brain naturally learns and applies these principles to increase the performance of agile teams.

#C1 - Connections

People do not learn with an empty head. They must connect the new topic with what they already know, what they want to learn, and also must connect with each other and create a safe environment.

Agile parallel: Aligning the team with project goals and vision. Sharing experiences of individual members and building psychological safety for open discussion.

#C2 - Concepts

Passive watching of PowerPoint presentations does not work. The brain receives information through multiple senses – listening, discussion, writing or reflection. People should share in pairs what they know, and the group validates it with each other.

Agile parallel: Breaking down vision into smaller parts (features/user stories) on cards that the whole team actively discusses, and regular daily synchronization (standups).

#C3 - Concrete Practice

The gap between understanding the concept and its real application must be closed as quickly as possible through active training. People learn by doing it or by teaching it to others ("when we teach, we learn").

Agile parallel: Learning the SCRUM framework by the team actually applying it to the project, or sharing knowledge through pair programming (extreme programming).

#C4 - Conclusions

The hardest phase of learning is retaining information. The conclusion forces participants to summarize what they learned, evaluate it and create a personal action plan for application in real life. Writing helps with this (e.g., learning logs).

Agile parallel: Agile retrospectives, where the team reflects on work and generates a concrete list of action steps for the next iteration.

#Example from practice: Closing the E2E knowledge gap in Flexibility Manager (4C Model)

  • C1 - Connections During root cause analyses of production issues in Flexibility Manager, I noticed that team members lacked understanding of the end-to-end flow across microservices. People were unsure about service ownership and how the chain of events worked. I gathered the RCA team members and started by asking each person to write down all the microservices they knew were involved in Flexibility Manager. This revealed the knowledge gaps immediately – everyone had a partial picture, but nobody held the complete chain. It also created a shared starting point and a safe space to admit what was unknown.

  • C2 - Concepts Using a concept board, I walked through the entire Flexibility Manager flow at a high level – from the problem domain (activity diagrams, domain modelling, NFRs) to the solution domain (microservice architecture, service ownership, Kafka topic interactions). We visualized how services like Circuit Optimizer, PSR Measurement, Case Manager, Proposal Creator, and Algorithmic Core interact through topics and REST/GraphQL endpoints. The goal was to give everyone a shared mental model of the full journey, not just their own service's slice.

  • C3 - Concrete Practice I set up simulated failure scenarios (e.g., "system is running but case is not created", "load assessment is not coming", "equipment violated but not considered for the proposal") and had teams work in groups to investigate them hands-on. Each group had to trace the issue through the microservice chain – checking simulator status, verifying published messages on Kafka topics, examining database state, and inspecting service logs. This forced them to practice observability and controllability across services they had never debugged before.

  • C4 - Conclusions At the end, I used a "Feel-O-Meter" to gauge each participant's confidence about the E2E process and flow. Participants also provided structured feedback on what went well and what could be improved. The confidence level had shifted noticeably toward the positive end compared to the start of the session. We captured the key findings and agreed on follow-up actions for areas that still needed deeper investigation.
